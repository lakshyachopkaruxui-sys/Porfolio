Research appendix

The course study used secondary research. The summaries below preserve its history, signals, forces, and scenario logic in readable form. They do not introduce primary research or reproduce unrecorded scoring.

1. History as context

The report traces four periods: adaptive agrarian practices; colonial changes in irrigation and extraction; Green Revolution intensification; and contemporary resource and technology pressures. This is the report’s interpretive historical sequence, not a claim that every region followed one identical path.

The design use is to question which relationships with water and work are being carried forward. Full account: original report §2.1.

2. Six signals considered in the report

Satellite crop monitoring
A shift toward remotely sensed information. Design question: who can interpret it and act on it?
AI-assisted yield forecasts
Prediction as a possible planning aid. Reported accuracy and lead-time figures are not retained without a specific evaluated model and dataset.
Drone scouting
Remote observation and intervention. Design question: who operates, maintains, and pays for the service?
AWD and monitoring
A soil-based water-management precedent behind field assistance. Carbon income is not assumed. IRRI guidance.
Soil and environmental sensors
More frequent feedback instead of occasional checks. The design still needs understandable information and reliable support.
SRI and direct seeding
Two distinct cultivation approaches grouped in the original scan. Their inclusion does not validate the proposed controlled-environment systems.

These are the course report’s selected signals, not proof that every technology is newly emerging. Source: report §§2.2–2.4. Unverified percentages and performance targets have been removed.

4. Drivers, uncertainty, and tensions

The report identifies seven drivers: labour shortage; rising input costs; water stress and climate variability; technological advancement; policy and institutions; market and income pressure; digital literacy and trust.

Its uncertainty discussion concentrates on water availability, adoption, and policy. No reproducible numeric scoring or weighting is supplied, so this revision does not invent ranks or scatterplot coordinates.

  • Technology adoption versus accessibility.
  • Sustainability versus economic practicality.
  • Policy support versus farmer autonomy.

Source: report §§3.3–3.4 and 4.1.

5. How to read the futures cone

Possible, plausible, and probable are nested judgements, not three independent bins. Preferable expresses a value choice that can overlap the categories; wildcards may disrupt different trajectories. The original project’s four narratives are illustrations, not calibrated probability estimates. Voros’s original explanation.

The preferred vision in report §4.5 prioritises understandable tools, local support, fair policy, and farmer participation. The concepts raise questions about that vision; they do not demonstrate that it has been achieved.

6. Evidence to design: a later portfolio addition

This traceability record was added during the portfolio revision on 16 November 2026. It is an editorial synthesis, not a recovered historical decision log.

Water-saving precedent → field assistance
Conditional AWD results support asking how guidance could be understandable and actionable in AI Smart Paddy. They do not establish this project’s savings or justify aeroponic yield claims. Carrijo et al.
Controlled-environment research → resource accounting
Rice growth in a plant factory gives a precedent for investigation. Vertical Paddy still needs full-cycle output, energy, cost, and resource assessment. Yang et al.
Rice–fish research → configuration matters
A trial with soil-filled pots cannot establish a soil-free loop. Aqua Rice requires its own nutrient, biological, and emissions tests. Suparmin et al.
Aeroponic delivery dependency → recovery requirements
Equipment and maintenance concerns motivate proposed backup, understandable alerts, repair access, and recovery behaviour. No failure-prediction accuracy or precise crop-survival window is claimed. van der Ent et al.
Preferred scenario → questions about agency
Report §4.5 motivates asking who can understand, consent, intervene, and obtain support. These questions are design criteria to investigate, not verified user needs from interviews.

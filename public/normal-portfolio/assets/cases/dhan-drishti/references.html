References and evidence boundaries

Literature check: 16 November 2026. These sources were checked during portfolio preparation. They are distinct from the original report bibliography; their inclusion does not mean they were consulted during the course.

IRRI / Rice Today (2009)

How much water does rice use?

Source type: Institutional explanation

Field water inputs include rainfall and irrigation; an Asian experimental average is about 2,500 L per kg of rough rice.

Boundary: Not a current India-specific rate or a direct benchmark for an untested chamber. Measurement boundaries must match.

IRRI (date not stated)

Alternate Wetting and Drying

Source type: Institutional guidance

AWD is an existing soil-based field-water practice.

Boundary: It does not establish aeroponic performance or guaranteed carbon-credit income.

Carrijo, Lundy & Linquist (2017)

Rice yields and water use under alternate wetting and drying irrigation: A meta-analysis

Source type: Peer-reviewed research synthesis

Mild AWD reduced total water input by 23.4% versus continuous flooding, without a significant yield reduction in most circumstances.

Boundary: Severe drying carried yield risk. Results are conditional and belong to the published studies, not this project.

Yang et al. (2026)

Machine-learning-assisted comparative analysis of rice growth and yield formation in field and plant factory systems

Source type: Peer-reviewed experimental study

Controlled-environment rice developed faster in the experiment, without a significant increase in final grain yield.

Boundary: Resource-use and economic indicators were incomplete; it does not validate the proposed stacked system.

Suparmin et al. (2025)

Pertumbuhan dan Hasil Empat Genotipe Padi (Oryiza Satifa L.) pada Sistem Budidaya Integrasi Padi-Ikan Secara Aquaponik

Source type: Peer-reviewed experimental study

Rice–fish cultivation was studied with and without additional NPK; some yield components benefited from supplementation.

Boundary: Soil-filled pots and catfish differ from the proposed soil-free system. No proof of zero fertiliser, zero methane, or higher income.

Chang et al. / IRRI (1991)

Cytogenetic, electrophoretic, and root studies of javanica rices, in Rice Genetics II

Source type: Rice research chapter; indexed passages checked

Aeroponic culture was used for comparative rice-root research.

Boundary: Root measurements do not establish full-cycle commercial grain production. Indexed publisher passages were checked; full-PDF retrieval timed out.

van der Ent et al. (2024)

Hydroponics in physiological studies of trace element tolerance and accumulation in plants focussing on metallophytes and hyperaccumulator plants

Source type: Peer-reviewed methodological review

Its aeroponics discussion identifies specialised equipment, high-pressure pumps, and mister-clogging disadvantages.

Boundary: Not a rice-yield trial; it does not establish a crop-death timeframe or validate predictive alerts.

Joseph Voros (2017)

The Futures Cone, use and history

Source type: Original author’s methodological explanation

Future categories are nested; preferable is a normative judgement that can overlap categories.

Boundary: Not a probability model or evidence that any project scenario will occur.
